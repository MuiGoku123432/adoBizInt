<objective>
Fold the two pacer warnings into PLAT-03 before the smoke re-run: give the request its own share of the deadline after a hold (WR-02, refining D-01's fit test) and bound any single hold (WR-03), with the D-08 message rounded so it never contradicts itself (IN-02). Record the review items the gap plans deliberately leave alone.

D-01 interpretation (locked decision, refined here, not contradicted): D-01 says the pacer fails fast when ADO's requested wait is longer than the time left before the request's deadline, and otherwise waits and then sends. Today "fits" means the hold ends before the deadline by any margin, so a 29s hold under a 30s budget sends the request with 1s left; it then times out, and a read reads timeout while a never-sent write could have been failed but reads unknown. That is the outcome D-01 exists to prevent ("waiting into a timeout hides the real cause", Pitfall 9). This plan reads "the wait fits" as: the hold ends with at least minSendBudget (2s) of the deadline left for the request itself, and the hold is no longer than maxHold (5m). Every refusal is still exactly the D-01 fail-fast (ErrRateLimited, rate_limit, the D-08 message, failed with no audit for a write under D-02, never retried under D-03). Every accepted wait is still honoured to the instructed time (D-04).

Authority for minSendBudget and the user's decision: the send budget is a cap on a single honoured Retry-After that fits the deadline, which 08-CONTEXT grants as Claude's Discretion ("any cap on a single honoured Retry-After that fits the deadline"). Because it narrows which waits D-01 honours, its size went to the user, who decided on 2026-10-21: "use 2s budget". Trade-off at the 30s default request_timeout: waits up to 28s are honoured and every request that is sent has at least 2s before its deadline; a 29s or 30s instruction (ADO documents delays up to 30s, Pitfall 9) fails fast as rate_limit instead of going out with 1s or less; a request_timeout at or below 2s fails every instructed wait fast. The 5s budget first proposed would have honoured waits only up to 25s and refused more of ADO's documented range; 2s honours more instructed waits at the cost of a smaller send window. The SUMMARY records this as a decision ("user, 2026-10-21: use 2s budget").

WR-03 decision, against D-01 to D-08: cap one hold at maxHold = 5m rather than reject request_timeout: 0 in Validate. Rejecting the zero value would remove a documented mode (large attachment downloads, internal/ado/attachments.go) and would still leave a user-configured long request_timeout able to hold silently for as long as ADO asks. The cap is the discretion 08-CONTEXT grants ("any cap on a single honoured Retry-After that fits the deadline"). It is not an invented throttle threshold under D-04: it never delays a request, it only refuses to hold one longer than ADO's own documented consumption window (200 TSTU per sliding 5 minutes, delays up to 30s; PITFALLS Pitfall 9, 08-RESEARCH). An instruction above the cap is kept as ADO sent it, so the tool never sends early; requests in that window fail fast as rate_limit with the asked time in the message (D-07: TUI shows it through the existing failure banner, D-05: a refusal is not a counted wait, D-08: Kind unchanged), and a restart clears it. That "notBefore never decays" consequence is accepted and recorded.

Supersession: maxHold reverses 08-RESEARCH Open Question 5, which resolved "no separate cap: the request deadline is the cap (D-01), and a fixed cap would be the invented threshold D-04 rules out". That resolution accepted the deadline-less startup case (request_timeout: 0) as unbounded; 08-REVIEW WR-03 showed it can park startup silently for as long as ADO asks. The reversal rests on the same 08-CONTEXT discretion grant quoted above and on the D-04 argument in the previous paragraph. 08-RESEARCH.md is the research record and is not edited; the supersession is stated here and in the SUMMARY decisions.
